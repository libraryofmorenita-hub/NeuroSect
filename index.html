The Science

Minority Report, grounded in engineering

The technology depicted in the 2002 film can be engineered using contemporary classical imaging techniques. fMRI, EEG, PPG, and MEG work in unison to create two clear reconstructions of brain activity — a delayed stream of all neuron movement on a 3D brain map, and video feedback of the subject's thoughts.

What the Physics Supports
Neural Encoding

Specific thoughts activate specific, repeatable brain patterns — stable enough across time to serve as individual neural fingerprints for decoder training.

Decoding

An AI trained on enough examples can learn to map activation patterns to semantic content. Demonstrated by UT Austin's non-invasive fMRI semantic decoder (Tang et al., Nature Neuroscience 2023).

Reconstruction

Latent diffusion models can synthesise visual scenes from encoded neural representations (Takagi & Nishimoto, 2023; Ozcelik & VanRullen, Scientific Reports 2023).

The Unsolved Engineering Problems
Temporal Mismatch

EEG resolves at milliseconds; fMRI at seconds. Real-time fusion across a three-order-of-magnitude gap is an unsolved systems engineering problem.

Signal-to-Noise Ratio

Brain magnetic fields at 10⁻¹³ T require SQUID sensors and extreme magnetic shielding to detect in a hospital EMI environment.

Imagination vs. Perception

Decoding predictive neural states requires training data for future-oriented brain activity — not present-time perception. No such dataset currently exists.

The Three Subjects

Alice, Ben, and Cal

Three genetically engineered subjects — developed from donor gametes via in vitro fertilisation and raised in sealed ectogenesis chambers with continuous neural modification. Their neurons are engineered to respond to transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS), driving synchronised theta-like states that enable detection of unresolved probability patterns.

Alice
Primary Hypothesis Channel

Produces visions in probabilistic order — her stream provides the primary semantic narrative and ordered ranking of candidate futures. Alice's larger hippocampal volume gives her fMRI stream the most reliable spatial anchor for Signal Separator isolation. Her neural state vector |ψ_A⟩ serves as the primary reference for the quantum probability calculator.

Ben
Independent Confirmatory Channel

Functions as an independent probabilistic corroborator. Ben's decoded output runs alongside Alice's and is held in reserve for the Consensus Engine. When his stream converges with Alice's, P(E|A,B,C) climbs. When he diverges, the system flags a minority report — a low-probability alternative future ranked separately from the consensus outcome.

Cal
Independent Confirmatory Channel

A second independent confirmatory stream whose randomised output, cross-referenced against Alice and Ben, determines final consensus ranking. When all three streams converge, confidence approaches certainty. The PPG signal from each precog acts as an arousal cross-check — unusual cardiac changes flag neural frames for exclusion from the predictive output.

Minority Report Flag

When Ben or Cal diverges from Alice entirely, the system flags a minority report — a low-probability alternative future preserved in the output and ranked separately from the consensus. The Consensus Engine produces a probability distribution, not a directive. Intervention remains a human decision made with full knowledge of the evidence.

The Multi-Modal Stack

Four modalities, one coherent signal

Each precog's head is housed inside a sealed neuro-interface helmet containing a coupling fluid — degassed, proton-poor, electrically insulating, and acoustically matched — engineered to support tFUS propagation without disturbing fMRI, EEG, or PPG signal acquisition.

fMRIFunctional Magnetic Resonance Imaging
1–2 sec temporal~1–2 mm spatial200–400 MB/s

Maps neuron activation through the BOLD (Blood Oxygen Level Dependent) haemodynamic response — a millimetre-precise proxy for neural activity. Each helmet contains its own head-specific receiver coil. A single high-field magnet images all three precogs simultaneously while the proton-poor coupling fluid contributes no competing MRI signal. The shared fMRI field provides the spatial foundation that makes per-subject Signal Separation possible.

EEGElectroencephalography
~1ms temporal256 channels6.1 MB/s

Captures electrical summation of neuron firing at millisecond resolution — detecting neural dynamics as they unfold rather than their vascular aftereffect. Electrodes placed directly on the scalp inside each helmet. The coupling fluid does not interfere. Targets delta (0.5–4 Hz), theta (4–8 Hz), alpha (8–12 Hz), beta (12–30 Hz), and gamma (30–100 Hz) frequency-band signatures for temporal pattern extraction.

MEGMagnetoencephalography
~1ms temporal306 channels9.8 MB/s

Reads magnetic fields produced by neural activation currents using SQUID sensors. Magnetic fields pass through the skull without distortion — giving MEG superior spatial resolution compared to EEG while maintaining the same millisecond temporal precision. NeuroSect uses EEG and MEG together because they capture the same neural event from different physical angles. While fMRI waits on the haemodynamic response, EEG and MEG have already caught the moment a candidate future emerges.

PPGPhotoplethysmography
~8ms temporal125 HzArousal proxy

Measures heart rate, heart rate variability, and pulse waveform morphology — reflecting autonomic state, cognitive load, and emotional arousal. In NeuroSect, PPG acts as an independent cross-check: unusual cardiac changes flag those neural frames for the AI pipeline to discard, keeping only clean data tied to genuine predictive episodes. PPG also removes heartbeat noise from the fMRI BOLD signal.

AI Decoding Pipeline

Five stages from raw signal to ranked future

The pipeline operates in five sequential stages. Each maintains human-in-the-loop decision making and closes the gap between raw neural data and human comprehension. The final output is a ranked probability distribution and a video reconstruction — never a directive.

01
Foundation
Subject-Specific Calibration

NeuroSect benefits from something no current decoder has — a lifetime of calibration data. Trained continuously from birth on spoken words, imagined stories, and everything in between. By operational maturity, individual decoders map each precog's neural signatures with precision far exceeding what a single session could achieve. Current semantic decoders require approximately 16 hours of individual training data per subject (Tang et al., 2023). A near-future medical protocol using spoken word decoding and passive resting state fingerprinting could compress this to under 15 minutes.

02
Stage 1
Signal Separator

Three brains, four imaging modalities, one shared scanner. The Signal Separator identifies, sorts, and delivers every incoming stream before a single candidate future can be decoded. The fMRI BOLD signal builds a continuous 3D spatial map of the shared environment — establishing each brain's precise anatomical location. A CNN pre-trained on each subject's calibration data assigns voxel-level BOLD activity to the correct precog with increasing accuracy. Alice's larger hippocampal volume provides the most reliable spatial anchor. Output: three separate, fully organised, time-aligned data packages.

CNN spatial fingerprintingMulti-subject fMRI isolationCross-modal time alignment
03
Stage 2
3D Pattern Decoder

Where raw neural data becomes legible. Two simultaneous outputs per precog: a live 3D brain map (fMRI BOLD via General Linear Model across all voxels in real time) and a continuous text stream describing the semantic content of each candidate future. A transformer-based language model trained on each precog's brain during calibration maps neural activation patterns to natural language — the same architecture UT Austin demonstrated for non-invasive continuous thought reconstruction. Alice's text stream is the primary ordered narrative. Ben's and Cal's streams are parallel probabilistic corroborators.

04
Stage 3
Image Synthesizer

The final translation layer between neural data and human comprehension. Built on Stable Diffusion — Takagi and Nishimoto (2023) demonstrated that fMRI BOLD contains sufficient information to condition a latent diffusion model into reconstructing accurate visual scenes, decoding semantic content, spatial layout, and depth simultaneously from neural activation. Alice is the primary source. Spatial features corroborated across all three streams are rendered at full probability weight; Alice-only features are flagged as unconfirmed. Scientists review the ranked text feed and make the call before any video is generated — preserving human judgment at every step.

05
Stage 4
Consensus Engine

Every candidate future passes through here before a frame of video is rendered. Three layers: neural feature extraction translates decoded outputs into comparable data structures; the quantum probability calculator maintains a library of all candidate futures and assigns probability amplitudes using Alice's neural state vector as the primary reference; the Bayesian consensus engine cross-references amplitudes against agreement across all three streams. Scientists receive the highest-probability candidate for video reconstruction plus a ranked text feed of the top fifteen alternative futures — similar outcomes with small detail differences, and futures that diverge entirely — preserving full probability landscape visibility.

The Mathematics

Bayesian consensus and quantum probability

The Consensus Engine operates in the same mathematical space as the probability amplitudes it is calculating — making it fast enough to keep pace with the continuous neural streams feeding in from all three precogs simultaneously.

Bayesian Consensus Formula
P(E | A, B, C) =
P(A|E) · P(B|E) · P(C|E) · P(E)

Why Quantum-Inspired Processing

Classical Bayesian inference becomes intractable above ~40 dimensions. NeuroSect's latent space operates in hundreds to thousands of dimensions. Tensor network methods — Matrix Product States and MERA decompositions — achieve better scaling than naive variational methods for structured high-dimensional probability distributions.

Signal Integrity

The engineering constraints

NeuroSect's signal integrity challenges are among the hardest in applied physics — extracting meaningful information from femtotesla-scale fields in a hospital EMI environment while sustaining 400 MB/s of raw data throughput across four asynchronous modalities.

10⁻¹³
Tesla · Brain Magnetic Field

MEG detects at the femtotesla scale — requiring SQUID sensors and full magnetic shielding. The neuro-interface helmet's coupling fluid is specifically engineered to improve ultrasound propagation without adding competing magnetic signal.

~1 μV
EEG Signal Amplitude

EEG amplitudes at the microvolt scale overlaid with power line interference, muscle artifacts, eye movement artifacts, and electrode contact noise — requiring ICA artifact removal before any decoding begins.

4–6 sec
fMRI Haemodynamic Lag

The BOLD response peaks four to six seconds after a neural event. Without EEG and MEG temporal bridging, fast-moving predictive activity could slip between hemodynamic snapshots entirely.

Why This Matters

Real applications, beyond PreCrime

The immediate future of NeuroSect is not law enforcement — it is medicine. The same multimodal pipeline that decodes three precogs could transform how neuroscience understands communication, disease, and the human brain.

Communication for Non-Verbal Patients

For patients with stroke, ALS, locked-in syndrome, or severe brain injury, NeuroSect could reconstruct the semantic and visual content of intended communication directly from brain activity — bypassing the motor pathway. Seeing a visual representation of what a patient is trying to express carries an immediacy that a synthesised sentence cannot replicate.

Psychiatric and Neurological Research

Psychiatry currently lacks lab values or neuroimaging to diagnose based on measurable metrics — relying on self-reporting limited by communication skill and willingness to disclose. NeuroSect could give scientists a direct representation of a patient's internal experience, enabling more objective diagnosis and treatment monitoring for schizophrenia, Alzheimer's, and mood disorders.

Law Enforcement and Investigation

NeuroSect could reconstruct a witness's memory for crime scene analysis, retrieve faces and names a witness cannot verbally articulate, and capture peripheral sensory details — background sounds, environmental cues — that a witness may have overlooked but that could narrow search areas in missing-person cases.

Multigenerational Neurogenetic Research

Scanning three generations of the same family simultaneously could reveal how neural signatures are inherited, modified, and expressed across lifetimes — tracking neurogenetic disorders live rather than reconstructing them from separate scans taken years apart. A day of NeuroSect imaging could replace years of sequential single-subject study.

Feasibility Assessment

Could this actually work?

What the Engineering Supports
Multi-modal fusion measurably reduces noise — each modality provides information the others cannot capture alone
Stable Diffusion already reconstructs visual scenes from fMRI BOLD signal with accuracy in semantic content, spatial layout, and depth (Takagi & Nishimoto, 2023)
Individual neural signatures are stable enough to serve as fingerprints — enabling per-subject decoder training
Non-invasive semantic decoding from fMRI demonstrated — UT Austin recovered continuous language from brain recordings without implants or speech
Live full-brain neural imaging demonstrated in living biological tissue at high spatial accuracy (MIT Picower Institute; UCLA International Brain Laboratory)
Where the Engineering Stops
Temporal mismatch — real-time fusion across the EEG-to-fMRI gap remains an unsolved systems engineering problem
Signal-to-noise — brain magnetic fields at 10⁻¹³ T require extreme shielding infrastructure not yet deployable in compact form
Predictive state decoding requires training data for future-oriented neural activity — no such dataset currently exists
The biological premise — genetically engineered quantum-thinking humans — is science fiction, not engineering
Raising humans in controlled environments for continuous cognitive use is impermissible under any current ethical framework

The components of a precognitive imaging system are not waiting to be invented. They are waiting to be assembled. Every component has a research team working on it right now. NeuroSect is the architecture that brings them together.
